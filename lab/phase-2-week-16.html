1Multi-AZ vs multi-region patterns; RTO/RPO tradeoffs

Four standard DR strategies, cheapest to most expensive
Backup & Restore     RTO: hours     RPO: hours     Just backups in another region, restore on demand
Pilot Light          RTO: ~10s min   RPO: minutes   Core services always running at minimal size
Warm Standby         RTO: minutes    RPO: seconds   Scaled-down full copy running, scale up on failover
Multi-Site Active/Active  RTO: near-zero  RPO: near-zero  Full capacity in 2+ regions simultaneously

RTO (Recovery Time Objective) is how long you can be down. RPO (Recovery Point Objective) is how much data you can afford to lose. Cost scales directly with how close to zero you push both — that tradeoff is the entire DR conversation with a business stakeholder.

2Design and diagram a DR strategy for a sample 3-tier app

Take the VPC + ALB/ASG + RDS stack you've built across Weeks 9-13 and design (on paper/diagram, not necessarily deployed) a Warm Standby DR strategy in a second region: what replicates continuously (RDS read replica, S3 cross-region replication), what's pre-provisioned but scaled down (a small ASG), and what a failover runbook does step by step.

A failover runbook skeleton — the kind of artifact SOA-C02 and DOP-C02 both expect later
1. Detect: Route 53 health check on primary region fails 3 consecutive checks
2. Promote: aws rds promote-read-replica --db-instance-identifier dr-region-replica
3. Scale: aws autoscaling update-auto-scaling-group --min-size 2 --desired-capacity 2 (DR region)
4. Redirect: Route 53 failover record automatically shifts traffic once health check flips
5. Verify: smoke-test the app in the DR region before declaring the incident resolved
6. Communicate: status update to stakeholders with current RTO/RPO actuals vs targets
Checkpoint

Commit the diagram and runbook to docs/architecture/dr-plan.md in your capstone repo — you'll expand on this directly in Phase 6's incident response work.
